# **Automated scaffolding inspection: a multi-modal strategy for joint fixity verification**

**Pure computer vision cannot reliably assess whether scaffolding joints are properly tightened** — this is the single most important finding shaping the FYP's technical direction. The literature confirms Prof. Xiang's caution: across all published research on bolt/fastener inspection, no method exists to determine tightness from images of unmarked couplers. As one industrial source puts it, "a loose bolt looks exactly the same as a tight bolt." [SmartBolts](https://smartbolts.com/insights/what-are-your-bolts-not-telling-you) This means joint fixity assessment — the core inspection task for metal scaffolding — demands a multi-modal approach combining geometric point cloud analysis, acoustic or vibration-based testing, and vision-based screening. This gap is also the team's greatest opportunity: **no commercial or academic system currently offers mobile, multi-modal scaffolding joint assessment**, making this a genuinely novel FYP contribution. The team's available hardware (iPad LiDAR, Intel RealSense, ARKit) is well-suited for this pivot, and Evan's reallocation from the now-deprioritized occlusion task creates capacity to tackle the project's hardest and highest-impact problem.

## **What vision can and cannot do for scaffolding joints**

YOLO-based scaffolding detection is maturing rapidly. The SADDS system (2024) uses YOLOv5 with HoloLens 2 to detect missing cross-tie rods, footboards, and bracing [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-5309/14/2/385) at **mAP 0.89**. [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-5309/14/2/385)[ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/377914139_A_Scaffolding_Assembly_Deficiency_Detection_System_with_Deep_Learning_and_Augmented_Reality) A 2025 system pairs YOLOv12 with Intel RealSense depth cameras to achieve **mAP 0.97** for mobile scaffolding safety violations, using a clever 3-second temporal trigger to suppress false alarms. [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/392888406_Computer_Vision-Based_Safety_Monitoring_of_Mobile_Scaffolding_Integrating_Depth_Sensors) Mask R-CNN approaches reach **F1 scores of 0.92** for scaffold safety monitoring. [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0166361521000555)[MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/12/19/10097) These results demonstrate that detecting the *presence or absence* of scaffolding components — including couplers — is a solved problem with adequate training data.

However, assessing joint *quality* from images remains fundamentally limited. The visual indicators of a loose coupler are extremely subtle: jaw gaps under 1mm (requiring feeler gauges to measure), bolt protrusion changes on the order of millimeters, and T-handle rotation angles that are nearly indistinguishable in photographs. [Yc-buildingmaterials](https://www.yc-buildingmaterials.com/technicaldocs/how_to_identify_and_deal_with_a_corroded_or_damaged_scaffold_coupler.html) All published bolt looseness detection methods that achieve meaningful accuracy require pre-applied markers — anti-loosening paint lines, circular markers, or hexagonal border analysis — none of which exist on standard scaffolding couplers. [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/22/14/5184) The most advanced marker-free approach uses multi-view camera fusion with rotated bounding boxes (2025), but even this requires controlled conditions and visible bolt geometry that scaffolding couplers don't provide. [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-5309/16/3/619)

What vision *can* reliably detect on couplers includes: missing coupler bolts/nuts (binary presence, AP \~0.93); [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10304286/) coupler type classification (right-angle, swivel, sleeve); surface corrosion via semantic segmentation (**F1 \~0.71** with Mask R-CNN, [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41529-022-00232-6) up to **98% recall** with ResNet-50 for binary classification); [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/12/19/10097) severe jaw deformation; and partially open couplers where the T-bolt is visibly not inserted. This makes vision an effective *screening layer* — flagging obviously defective joints — but not a definitive tightness assessment tool.

**A critical dataset gap exists**: no published scaffolding dataset covers coupler types, conditions, and component-level annotation. Building this dataset will be a necessary team investment, likely requiring **300-500+ annotated images** of Hong Kong tube-and-coupler scaffolding joints captured at close range from inside the scaffold.

## **Multi-modal approaches that can actually assess joint tightness**

Three sensor modalities show genuine promise for supplementing vision-based joint assessment, ranked by feasibility for a student project:

**Acoustic tap testing** is the most immediately implementable approach. A 2023 ScienceDirect paper demonstrates percussion-based bolt looseness detection using "voiceprint features" — essentially digitizing the traditional ring test where inspectors tap connections and listen for rattling. The principle: tight joints produce a clear, high-frequency acoustic signature, while loose joints produce lower-frequency, damped responses. This can be implemented with the iPad's built-in microphone, a standardized tapping tool (a small hammer), and ML classifiers applied to audio spectrograms. Hardware cost is effectively zero. The team would need to collect a labeled dataset of tap sounds on joints at various torque levels, then train a classifier — a well-scoped ML task.

**Vibration-based looseness detection** is the most scientifically validated method. A 2025 PMC paper demonstrates that the "Summation Coefficient of Absolute Spectrum Ratio" (SCASR) shows an **excellent linear relationship with bolt torque**, detecting even early-stage loosening. [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11723463/) Multiple studies confirm that ML classifiers applied to accelerometer data can distinguish 6 N·m torque decrements on bolts. [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/22/3/1210) The challenge: these methods require **5-10 kHz sampling rates**, far beyond the iPad's MEMS accelerometer (100-400 Hz). The iPad IMU could detect gross scaffold sway or comparative vibration differences between sections, but individual joint assessment would require an external MEMS accelerometer (\~$50-200). This is a stretch goal but technically well-supported.

**Thermal imaging with active excitation** is a novel approach validated by a groundbreaking 2025 paper from Chaoyang University of Technology. Under mechanical vibration, insufficiently pre-tensioned bolts generate more microscopic friction and greater temperature rise in contact areas, creating a **clear positive correlation between torque and thermal response**. [sciforumSciforum](https://sciforum.net/manuscripts/23352/abstract_pdf.pdf) A FLIR ONE Pro (\~$300-500) connects to the iPad via USB-C, provides 160×120 thermal resolution, [Test Equipment Depot](https://www.testequipmentdepot.com/teledyne-flir-flir-one-pro-ios-thermal-imaging-camera-attachment-lightning-connector-onefit-msx-and-vividir-for-ios-devices-160-x-120-9-hz.html)[Legatool](https://legatool.com/en/flir-one-pro-ios-thermal-imaging-camera-attachment-for-ios) and has an SDK for custom iOS integration. The limitation: this requires controlled vibration excitation and close-range imaging, making it more suitable as a complementary confirmation tool than a primary screening method.

## **LiDAR-based geometric compliance is a high-value, achievable goal**

Point cloud analysis for verifying scaffolding geometry against dimensional standards is both technically feasible and highly valuable. The iPad Pro's dToF LiDAR achieves **±1-2 cm accuracy** in favorable conditions, [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666165923000510) sufficient to verify member spacing (standard intervals of 1.2-2.0m), plumbness/verticality of standards, and levelness of horizontal members. Intel RealSense D435 offers better close-range detail but requires external computing.

For scaffolding member extraction, **RANSAC cylinder fitting** is the established approach — PCL provides `SACMODEL_CYLINDER` out of the box. [Point Cloud Library](https://pointclouds.org/documentation/group__sample__consensus.html) The workflow involves estimating surface normals, iteratively fitting cylinders (48.3mm diameter for standard scaffolding tubes), and extracting member centerlines. A key challenge is that iPad LiDAR's point density may be marginal for reliable cylinder fitting on 48mm-diameter tubes; [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/14/24/11814) using **line fitting on centerlines** with assumed standard tube diameters is a pragmatic alternative. Deep learning approaches are also emerging: **RandLA-Net** achieves **93.67% accuracy** for scaffolding point cloud segmentation across six construction sites, and panoptic segmentation methods reach **83.51% AP@50** with shape-aware constraints for slender scaffolding elements. [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S092658052400339X)

The geometric compliance checks map directly to Hong Kong's Code of Practice for Metal Scaffolding Safety (Labour Department, 2nd Edition, March 2013\) and the Construction Sites (Safety) Regulations. [labour](https://www.labour.gov.hk/eng/public/os/B/mss.pdf) Key verifiable parameters include guard-rail height (**900-1150mm**), working platform width (**≥400mm**), [Hkicm](https://hkicm.org.hk/pdf/HKICM-PNCM8-TW03.pdf) platform gaps (**≤25mm**), [Housingauthority](https://www.housingauthority.gov.hk/mini-site/site-safety/common/resources/whats-new/201603/ssip_guidelines_6th_en.pdf) bracing continuity from base to top, [Hkicm](https://hkicm.org.hk/pdf/HKICM-PNCM8-TW03.pdf) and tie spacing (**\<5.5m vertical, \<7.5m horizontal**). [Buildings Department](https://www.bd.gov.hk/doc/en/resources/codes-and-references/code-and-design-manuals/GDCBS.pdf)[LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/inspection-tube-fitting-type-scaffolding-hasan-shirazi-pe-bsc-ceng) An automated system that compares scanned geometry against these thresholds would directly support the mandatory **fortnightly Form 5 inspections** required under CSSR Regulation 38F. [Labour Department](https://www.labour.gov.hk/eng/public/os/B/mss.pdf)

## **From point clouds to structural capacity estimates**

The connection between 3D scan data and structural analysis is well-established in research, following the pipeline: point cloud → member extraction → structural graph → beam-column FEA model → capacity assessment. Semi-rigid joint modeling is **critical** for scaffolding analysis — couplers are neither fully pinned nor fully fixed, [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0141029609004210) with bending stiffness typically ranging from **5 to 100+ kN·m/rad** depending on coupler type and tightness. Research shows that when joint stiffness exceeds approximately **2.0 times the reference stiffness**, structural capacity plateaus [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0308016122002162) — meaning there's a threshold below which looseness significantly affects safety.

For a student project, the recommended approach uses simplified beam-column models rather than full nonlinear FEA. Open-source Python libraries like **PyNite** or **OpenSeesPy** can model scaffolding frames with rotational springs at joints, apply standard loads (self-weight, imposed loads, wind), and compute member utilization ratios. The key innovation would be feeding *measured* geometric imperfections from LiDAR scans (out-of-plumb, out-of-straightness) into the model rather than using code-prescribed default values. Research consistently shows scaffolding is "imperfection sensitive" — actual as-built geometry can significantly reduce capacity versus idealized designs. [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/343360349_Stability_design_of_system_scaffold_with_differential_settlement) The sensitivity hierarchy is generally: **out-of-plumb \> out-of-straightness \> connection looseness**, meaning LiDAR-detected geometric deviations are actually more safety-critical than vision-detected joint conditions.

Commercial scaffolding analysis tools (STAAD Pro, SAP2000, SkyCiv) exist but are expensive and not designed for scan-data input. **Cloud2FEM**, an open-source Python tool, converts point clouds to FE meshes and could be adapted for scaffolding. The ambitious version of this module would generate automated structural adequacy reports, while the minimum viable version would flag members or bays where measured geometry deviates beyond code-permitted tolerances.

## **The commercial landscape reveals a clear opportunity**

**No mobile, AI-powered scaffolding inspection system exists commercially.** Current commercial solutions fall into two categories, both with significant gaps:

Digital scaffold management platforms — Avontus ScaffoldIQ, [Avontus](https://www.avontus.com/scaffoldiq/) SMART Scaffolder, Scafflinq, Cloudscaff [Cloudscaff](https://cloudscaff.com/products/daily-inspections) — provide workflow management, digital checklists, and QR-code-based scaffold tracking. [Scafflinq \+3](https://scafflinq.com/) None incorporate computer vision, LiDAR scanning, or automated defect detection. They digitize the *paperwork* of inspection, not the *assessment* itself.

The only AI-vision company in the space is **viAct.ai** (Hong Kong-based), whose Smart Site Safety System (4S) is pre-approved by Hong Kong's Development Bureau. However, viAct connects to fixed CCTV cameras for worker safety monitoring (PPE, danger zones) — it does not perform structural assessment of scaffolding components or joints, and it doesn't offer mobile/tablet-based inspection. [Viact](https://www.viact.ai/solutions)[Viact](https://www.viact.ai/smart-site-safety-system)

This gap means a mobile system that combines on-device YOLO detection, LiDAR geometric compliance checking, and multi-modal joint assessment would be genuinely novel — not just academically, but commercially. Hong Kong's Construction 2.0 initiative, [The Standard](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/breaking-news/section/4/204293/The-CIC-launches-CDE-Award-to-promote-digitalization-in-the-construction-industry) the Construction Innovation and Technology Fund (CITF), [Cic](https://www.cic.hk/eng/main/industry_development/) and the CIC's designation of construction safety as a priority all create a receptive ecosystem for this type of tool. [Viact](https://www.viact.ai/post/scaffolding-safety-in-hong-kong-cic-and-oshc-leading-the-path)

## **Proposed team restructuring: four roles optimized for joint fixity**

Given that occlusion handling is deprioritized, the team's effort should redistribute toward the multi-modal joint fixity assessment challenge. Here is the recommended role assignment, with technical justification for each:

**Evan → Multi-Modal Joint Fixity Assessment Lead.** This is the project's highest-impact and most technically demanding role, appropriate for the strongest team member. Evan builds the "Joint Assessment Module" — a multi-modal pipeline that fuses three signals to classify joint condition:

* *Geometric signal*: Extract coupler geometry from RealSense depth data at close range (gap measurements, bolt protrusion, jaw opening angle). RealSense's sub-2mm depth accuracy at close range exceeds iPad LiDAR for this task. Evan would implement cylinder-pair intersection analysis to detect whether tubes are properly seated in coupler jaws.  
* *Acoustic signal*: Build a tap-test classifier using iPad microphone input. Collect a labeled dataset of tap sounds on couplers at various torque levels (ideally with a calibrated torque wrench for ground truth). Train a spectrogram-based CNN (could reuse a lightweight model architecture). This is the project's most novel technical contribution.  
* *Fusion architecture*: Design a feature-level fusion module that combines visual features (from Chloe's detector), geometric features, and acoustic features into a joint condition score. A cross-attention mechanism or simpler weighted voting system would work.  
* *Stretch goal*: Integrate a FLIR ONE thermal camera for active thermography experiments on joints under vibration excitation.

**Chloe → Scaffolding Component Detection and Visual Joint Screening.** Chloe continues with YOLO-based detection but refocuses scope. Rather than microscopic defect detection (which the literature shows is unreliable at working distances), Chloe focuses on:

* *Component detection*: Train a custom YOLO model (v8 or v11, nano variant for on-device performance) to detect and classify scaffolding components in Hong Kong metal scaffolding — standards, ledgers, transoms, braces, base plates, toe boards, and critically, **couplers by type** (right-angle, swivel, sleeve). This requires building a custom dataset of \~500+ annotated images from Hong Kong construction sites.  
* *Visual defect screening*: Detect obviously defective conditions — missing components, visible corrosion (binary classification), severely damaged couplers, missing coupler bolts. Use SAHI (Slicing Aided Hyper Inference) for detecting small couplers in wider field-of-view images.  
* *Joint region proposals*: Chloe's detector outputs bounding boxes around detected couplers, which serve as input regions for Evan's multi-modal assessment module. This creates a natural handoff: Chloe identifies *where* joints are; Evan assesses *how tight* they are.  
* *CoreML export*: Ensure models run on-device at 30+ FPS via CoreML with FP16 quantization. [Ultralytics](https://docs.ultralytics.com/integrations/coreml/)

**Saanvi → LiDAR Geometric Compliance and Structural Analysis.** Saanvi's macroscopic analysis role expands to encompass the full scan-to-assessment pipeline:

* *Point cloud processing*: Implement the scaffolding member extraction pipeline using Open3D — preprocessing (noise removal, voxel downsampling), RANSAC line/cylinder fitting for tube detection, [Open3D](https://www.open3d.org/docs/release/tutorial/geometry/pointcloud.html) and member connectivity graph generation. Use iPad LiDAR for bay-level scanning and RealSense for higher-detail captures.  
* *Geometric compliance engine*: Build automated checks against Hong Kong Code of Practice requirements — guard-rail height (900-1150mm), platform width (≥400mm), [Hkicm](https://hkicm.org.hk/pdf/HKICM-PNCM8-TW03.pdf) member spacing, plumbness tolerances, bracing continuity. Output a compliance scorecard per bay/section.  
* *Simplified structural model*: Generate beam-column models from extracted geometry using PyNite or OpenSeesPy. Apply prescribed loads and semi-rigid joint stiffness values from literature. Compute utilization ratios and identify over-stressed members or stability-critical geometric deviations.  
* *Key deliverable*: A "digital twin comparison" view showing as-built geometry overlaid on code-compliant reference geometry, with deviations color-coded by severity.

**Shalini → iOS Platform, Sensor Integration, and Inspection Workflow.** Shalini's integration role becomes even more critical with the multi-modal architecture:

* *ARKit \+ CoreML pipeline*: Build the core iOS app using ARKit for LiDAR capture and spatial mapping, [In the Scan](https://blog.lidarnews.com/3d-lidar-based-app-announced-by-apple/) CoreML/Vision framework for running Chloe's YOLO model in real-time, [Ultralytics](https://docs.ultralytics.com/integrations/coreml/) and RealityKit for AR overlay of detection results and compliance annotations.  
* *Sensor orchestration*: Manage data flow from multiple sensors — iPad camera (RGB), iPad LiDAR (depth/mesh), Intel RealSense (high-res depth for joint inspection), iPad microphone (tap test audio), and potentially FLIR ONE (thermal). Build a unified data capture session that timestamps and spatially registers all sensor inputs.  
* *Inspection workflow UX*: Design the inspector's workflow — bay-by-bay scanning, automatic component checklist generation, joint-by-joint assessment with guided prompts ("tap this coupler now"), and **automated Form 5 report generation** for Hong Kong's mandatory fortnightly inspections. [Labour Department](https://www.labour.gov.hk/eng/public/os/B/mss.pdf)  
* *Data management*: Offline-first architecture (critical for construction sites with poor connectivity), local SQLite/Core Data storage with cloud sync, and export to standard formats (PDF reports, PLY point clouds, JSON inspection records).

## **Conclusion: the multi-modal pivot changes everything**

The professor's insight eliminates a moderate-difficulty task (occlusion handling) and redirects the team toward the project's true technical frontier: **can a mobile system reliably assess scaffolding joint fixity without a torque wrench?** The answer from the literature is "not with vision alone, but plausibly with sensor fusion." The acoustic tap test approach is the team's most novel and publishable contribution — it has minimal hardware requirements, clear scientific grounding in vibration-based looseness detection research, and no existing implementation for scaffolding couplers. Combined with close-range geometric analysis from RealSense depth data and visual screening via YOLO, this creates a three-layer assessment system: vision screens for obviously defective joints, geometry checks for dimensional deviations, and acoustics probes for tightness — each layer catching what the others miss.

The restructuring concentrates the team's effort on a coherent pipeline where each member's output feeds the next: Shalini captures multi-sensor data → Chloe detects and localizes components → Evan assesses joint condition → Saanvi evaluates structural adequacy. This replaces the previous structure (where occlusion handling was somewhat isolated) with a tightly integrated chain where every role is load-bearing. The project's positioning shifts from "scaffolding defect detection" (crowded, incremental) to "multi-modal joint fixity assessment" (novel, directly responsive to a real inspection limitation) — a stronger thesis for both the FYP evaluation and any potential publication or CITF funding application.
